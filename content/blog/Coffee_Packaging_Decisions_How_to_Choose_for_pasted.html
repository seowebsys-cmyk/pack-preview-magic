Start with the freshness protection your coffee needs

At RoasterPack.com, we believe great coffee deserves great packaging. Oxygen speeds oxidation and the loss of aroma, while a moisture barrier helps keep dry coffee from absorbing moisture; light and heat can also speed quality decline.

Freshly roasted whole beans release carbon dioxide, so a one-way degassing valve can let that gas escape while limiting air entry. It supports freshness, but it cannot replace effective barrier material or a sound seal.

Ground coffee has more exposed surface area than whole beans and usually loses aroma faster. Compare packaging against the coffee’s roast-to-sale timing and distribution conditions.

RoasterPack offers high-barrier materials, resealable zippers and degassing valves as options for custom coffee packaging. Test filled, sealed bags through realistic storage and transit conditions, checking seal integrity, visible damage and coffee quality over time.

  • Keep samples from the start and end of a test run to compare how quality changes.
  • Include the actual shipping carton and handling route in transit tests.
  • Assess filled packages, not just a material description or an empty pouch.

Compare material structures and sustainability tradeoffs

Kraft paper gives a natural paper look, while coffee packaging commonly combines an outer paper layer with an inner barrier layer that limits oxygen and moisture exposure.

Material or structureWhat it means for your packaging choice
Foil-lined or multilayer filmCan provide strong barrier protection. Compare its performance with the coffee’s storage duration and the needs of your shipping and retail channels.
Recyclable mono-material filmUses a single material structure designed for recycling, but practical end-of-life options depend on the specific material and local collection systems.
Compostable packagingDesigned to break down under composting conditions. Access to an appropriate composting facility matters, and compostable does not mean recyclable.
Biodegradable packagingDescribes material breakdown through biological processes. It does not mean the same thing as compostable, and the conditions for breakdown matter.

Recyclable, compostable and biodegradable describe different end-of-life outcomes. Choose a barrier structure that protects the coffee, then check whether customers can realistically use the disposal route associated with its material claim.

Packed for the planet. Sustainability and freshness work best as part of the same decision: select material for the coffee’s shelf life and distribution, then weigh the disposal options available where customers live.

Check features, size and production compatibility

A resealable zipper helps customers close a bag after opening and can limit further air exposure. It does not compensate for weak barrier material or a poor initial seal.

Consider nitrogen flushing when reducing oxygen in the filled package is important. It requires compatible filling and sealing equipment, and its value depends on the bag’s barrier performance as well as the product’s roast-to-sale timing.

Match bag dimensions and fill weight to the coffee and your filling equipment. Check that your sealing method and any valve placement work with the production line before approving a format.

Make the design work for the customer and the shelf

Your coffee tells a story, and clear packaging helps customers understand what they are choosing. Make the coffee’s identity easy to scan on the front, while keeping product information legible and distinct from decorative artwork.

Review artwork at the intended package size. Small text, low contrast and details placed close to folds or seals can become difficult to read on a finished bag.

  • Check that the coffee name and key identifying details are easy to find.
  • Look for text that crosses folds, gussets, seals or curved surfaces.
  • Assess how the package opens, reseals and stores after purchase.

Test the design on a filled bag in its intended display setting. That final check shows whether the artwork, package shape and shelf footprint work together.

Plan a custom order and repeat run carefully

Before committing, align bag dimensions, material, fill quantity, sealing method and valve requirements with your filling process. This practical check helps prevent an artwork approval or packaging order from getting ahead of production constraints.

Review a packaging proof, approve a physical sample and account for applicable labeling requirements before production. Confirm the minimum order quantity and production schedule as part of the same planning conversation.

Plan stock around your launch, track how much you will need before the next run and keep contingency stock where a delay would interrupt sales.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should freshly roasted coffee degas before it is sealed?

Freshly roasted coffee needs time to degas before sealing; roast level and intended brew method affect the ideal rest. Espresso is often rested longer than filter coffee to let its flavour settle.

Is vacuum sealing suitable for freshly roasted coffee beans?

Vacuum sealing immediately after roasting can compress beans while they are still releasing carbon dioxide. For freshly roasted coffee, use a packaging process designed to manage degassing rather than applying a household vacuum seal without testing it.

Can coffee bags be reused for another batch?

Reuse only a clean, food-safe bag with an intact closure and no lingering coffee oils or odours. Flexible pouches can be difficult to clean fully, so fresh packaging is the more dependable choice for preserving a new batch’s flavour.

How should an opened bag of coffee be stored?

Close the package tightly and keep it in a cool, dry cupboard away from direct sunlight and strong-smelling foods. Avoid repeatedly moving the coffee between cold and warm environments, which can encourage condensation.

Should I use an oxygen absorber in a coffee bag?

An oxygen absorber can reduce residual oxygen in a sealed package, but it must be suited to the packaging system and the product’s gas release. Test it with the complete filled package to confirm that it works alongside the barrier and closure.
